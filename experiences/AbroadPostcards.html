Personal Abroad

Documenting my Semester Abroad

Madrid, Spain · Fall 2026 · Personal Exploration

Studying abroad in Madrid has given me the opportunity to explore far beyond the classroom. Throughout the semester, I have been documenting the places I visit, the things I learn, and the experiences that shape my time in Spain and around Europe.

Rather than keeping a traditional travel diary, I am using this space to push my creative processes, using art as a way to step back, slow down, and truly reflect on these experiences. Each piece is a creative pause button—an intentional effort to transform my memories into a living work of art.

Week 01 — Segovia
Week 1
A class field trip to segovia

My first week having arrived in Madrid, we took a field trip to Segovia. We took a two-hour bus ride to Segovia, stepping off into a town that immediately felt like a step back in time. Our day began with a guided tour of the city, where we learned about the incredible strength and history of the Roman aqueduct. Our guide shared the old local legend of how it came to be—a tale about a young woman who made a desperate deal with the devil to deliver water to the city just to ease her daily chores.

From there, we visited the town's majestic castle, "Alcazar de Segovia". Moving through the fortress, we learned the history behind every single room. It is pretty incredible how much much space the royalty had all to themselves. I think I would be overwhelemed. For the first time since arriving in Spain, this felt like a true study abroad experience. I absolutely loved that the streets were free of cars, and though it was certainly touristy, the entire atmosphere felt completely unlike anything I could ever find back home in the USA.

Before we left, our guide recommended we try a local sweet cake called Ponche Segoviano. Being a huge fan of dessert, I absolutely loved its rich sweetness, though a few people in our group found it a bit too sugary for their taste. We wrapped up the afternoon with a class lunch of traditional Spanish tapas before heading back. I am so incredibly grateful that my program was able to take us to Segovia to truly kick off my adventure in Spain and beyond!

Week 02 — Valencia, Spain
Week 2
Madrid Architecture

Summary of Valencia

Three friends and I decided to take advantage of Spain’s train system and escape to the coast for a trip to Valencia. We reserved beds in a hostel and boarded a train early in the morning. Upon arriving two hours later, it was too early to check in, so we took our bags and explored the Old Town. We saw the food markets, the Church of San Nicolás, and accidentally joined a tour of a tower entirely in Spanish. It’s times like this when I’m truly excited for my Spanish lessons to begin. Though I didn’t understand many of the words, it was awesome to climb these historic towers and look out over the town. Valencia feels much more “European” than Madrid in my opinion, at least in the Old Town, with its cobblestone streets and perfectly curated oranges and yellows of the buildings.

That evening, we tried paella for the first time. Valencian paella contains rabbit, and I thought it was great. We also saw the neon lights at the City of Arts and Sciences, where an outdoor venue transforms into a nightclub at dusk.

The next day, we spent some more time exploring and rented bikes to cycle through the villages. We stopped at the beach and took some much-needed time to stop moving and relax. That night, we ate at a small restaurant in the Old Town, had some more paella—this time seafood—and enjoyed each other’s company. It was great getting to know the girls I had traveled with and starting some great relationships for our time abroad.
Week 03 — Ibizia
Week 3
A once in a lifetime trip to Ibizia

Everyone I’ve spoken to from years past talked about how one of their most surreal experiences abroad was Ibiza, so this weekend it seemed like everyone I knew studying in Europe had made their way to this Spanish island. Upon arriving, I was pleasantly surprised by how beautiful the coast was. The songs made me think it was an island purely for parties, and while there was certainly much to do there, the beaches were the best I’ve ever seen.

I had the most incredible experience listening to DJ sets that I’ve heard songs written about and spending time with friends. It is true that people do not sleep much in Ibiza, and this will be a once-in-a-lifetime experience for me, but I am so grateful I had the opportunity to go!

Week 04 — Florence
Week 4
The beauty of Florence

This trip was very last minute, and a reminder that there is studying in stufy abroad!

Here is where I ate my best meals. For lunch, I had a focaccia sandwich with mortadella, stracciatella, and pesto. It forever changed my perspective on sandwiches and their potential. This trip was very last minute, and a reminder that there is studying in study abroad! We spent some time doing work together at a local café, and then I walked off on my own to take in the streets of Florence. I got to practice the Italian I had been holding onto from high school and spoke with local artists on the streets. Around every corner, there was someone painting, drawing, or singing. “Sarà perché ti amo” seemed to live in the walls.

That night, we watched the sunset at Piazzale Michelangelo with some friends, and I witnessed an entire community join in song as the sun set.

The next day, my friend and I jumped on a wine tour, learning about the history of Chianti Classico wine in the Italian countryside. I learned that I am not a huge fan of good-quality wine, and the small palate cleansers of cheese and meat were more my style. However, the countryside was beautiful. It is amazing how just a short drive outside of a stone city, you can be surrounded by nothing but nature.

On Sunday, I attended Mass at the Duomo in Italian, did some more work with the most incredible views of Florence, and took some time to appreciate how wonderful this little walkable city is.
