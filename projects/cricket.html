Reptile Release Feeder — Cricket Crew

The Reptile Release Feeder is designed to slowly dispense crickets over a two-hour window to mimic natural prey distribution and discourage rapid feeding or competition with birds. The system emphasizes durability, ease of maintenance, and low cost so it can be replicated across habitats. Design criteria (capacity, timing, safety, run-time, and maintenance) were developed with the client and captured in the team memo. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

Our prototype holds approximately 150 crickets, supports randomized or staggered release timing, and uses a low-noise, low-visibility mechanism to remain unobtrusive for zoo visitors while providing a reliable feeding experience for keepers.

Project Demo

Demo overview

The demo shows the hopper dispensing crickets at randomized intervals (configurable between ~2–10 minutes) and the unit operating quietly for extended periods. Timing is handled by a simple microcontroller-based scheduler that triggers short actuation pulses to avoid crushing prey.

Design priorities

  • Cricket survival: internal temperature and gentle release ensure crickets survive the dispense interval.
  • Randomized release: timing variability to simulate natural prey behavior and reduce predictability.
  • Keeper usability: easy-fill hopper and tool-less disassembly for cleaning in under five minutes.

Features

Release Mechanism
Low-impact actuator dispenses crickets either randomly or staggered over time.

The dispensing mechanism is designed to push small batches of crickets out at short intervals (configurable via onboard controls). It uses a gentle rotor wheel with cups sized to hold a few crickets each, minimizing crushing and ensuring live prey are delivered intact.

Key points:

  • Adjustable dispense volume per actuation
  • Random timing between 2–10 minutes (client spec).
  • Actuation pulse short enough to avoid damaging crickets.
Hopper & Capacity
Holds ~150 crickets, easy to refill and clean.

The hopper is sized to meet client capacity (~150 crickets) and is built with smooth internal surfaces to prevent jams and to allow quick hand-cleaning. The feed angle and outlet design prevent cricket climbing and accidental escapes inside the housing.

Timing Logic & Electronics
Microcontroller schedules randomized/staggered release and power management.

A small MCU handles actuator timing, adjustable release patterns, and battery monitoring. Safety interlocks prevent actuation during hopper access. Power management is sized to run at least 14 hours per charge per client requirement.

  • Configurable randomization window (2–10 min)
  • Battery life target: 14 hours on one charge (design goal)
  • Protected connectors for easy maintenance
Thermal Control & Cricket Survival
Passive thermal design plus optional heater to keep crickets healthy.

The internal environment is managed to keep crickets in the 22–32°C range to ensure survival for the release window, using passive insulation and, where necessary, low-power heaters controlled by a thermostat.

Maintenance & Cleaning
Tool-less disassembly for fast cleaning and service.

The feeder is designed for quick field servicing: the hopper and rotor sub-assembly remove without tools and can be cleaned in under 10 minutes, meeting the team's maintainability goals.

Safety & Appearance
No openings <1" and discrete aesthetics for visitor experience.

All openings exceed the no-less-than-1-inch safety constraint and interior geometry avoids sharp corners. The housing is acoustically damped to be visually and audibly unobtrusive in the exhibit.
